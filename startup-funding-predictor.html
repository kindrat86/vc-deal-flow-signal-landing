Methodology

Commit Velocity

Measures the rate of change in commit frequency over a 14-day rolling window. A sudden 3× or greater increase signals that the engineering team has been staffed up or reprioritized — typically a precursor to fundraising.

Contributor Growth

Tracks the number of unique contributors committing to the organization's repos. Sustained contributor growth over 4+ weeks correlates strongly with pre-fundraise team expansion.

Repository Expansion

New public repositories being created and actively developed. New repos suggest product expansion, which often accompanies or precedes a capital raise.

Signal Type Classification

We classify each acceleration into one of four archetypes: deploy frequency spike (tooling/process), engineering hiring burst (headcount), framework migration (technical debt cleanup), or infrastructure buildout (scaling). Each has a different lead-time profile.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does engineering velocity predict fundraising timing?

GitDealFlow research shows a 21–47 day lead window between detectable engineering acceleration (commit velocity spikes, contributor growth, repo expansion) and public fundraise announcements. Startups that suddenly accelerate their engineering output are typically preparing for a raise — hiring engineers, building new features, and scaling infrastructure ahead of investor demos.

What signals does the funding predictor use?

The predictor analyzes four signal types: commit velocity trend (how fast commits are growing), contributor growth rate (team expansion), repository expansion (new products/projects), and signal type classification (deploy frequency spike, engineering hiring burst, framework migration, or infrastructure buildout). Learn more about deal flow signals.

How accurate is the funding prediction?

Our model has been validated against historical fundraise announcements in our dataset. The 21–47 day lead window reflects observed patterns across 4,200+ tracked startups. Accuracy varies by sector and company maturity — pre-seed and seed-stage startups show the strongest correlation between velocity changes and fundraising timing.

Can founders use this to time their fundraise?

Absolutely. Founders can check their startup's velocity signals on GitDealFlow's Velocity Checker to understand how their engineering activity compares to startups that raised recently. A rising velocity trend over 2–4 weeks suggests strong fundraising positioning. We recommend maintaining or increasing velocity for at least 3 weeks before starting the fundraise process.
