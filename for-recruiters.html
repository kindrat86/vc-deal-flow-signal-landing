Engineering Team Growth Signals

GitDealFlow identifies startups that are actively scaling their engineering teams through two primary signals:

Hiring Burst Signal

When a startup adds 5+ new contributors to its public GitHub repositories within a 14-day window, we flag it as a hiring burst. This is the strongest leading indicator that the company is actively onboarding new engineering talent — often before the roles are posted on LinkedIn or company career pages.

Deploy Frequency Spike

A sudden increase in deploy frequency suggests the team is shipping more code, which often correlates with new team members ramping up. Deploy spikes typically precede hiring burst signals by 2–3 weeks, giving recruiters an even earlier window into startup growth phases.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you identify startups that are hiring engineers? ▼

GitDealFlow detects hiring bursts by monitoring contributor growth on public GitHub repositories. A startup that suddenly adds 5+ new contributors to its repos in a 14-day window is flagged with a 'Hiring Burst' signal. We also track deploy frequency spikes, which often precede public job postings by 2–3 weeks.

Which sectors have the most hiring activity right now? ▼

Based on current data, the most active hiring sectors include healthcare, enterprise SaaS, data infrastructure, and fintech. You can browse sector-specific hiring activity on our sector pages and filter by hiring burst signals.

Is the recruiter data free to use? ▼

Yes. GitDealFlow offers a permanent free tier that includes access to the hiring burst signals board, trending startups, scout score, and API access. No credit card or signup required for basic access.

Can I filter startups by location for recruiting purposes? ▼

Yes. GitDealFlow tracks startup locations across 7 geographies including United States, Europe, APAC, United Kingdom, LATAM, Canada, and specific cities like San Francisco, New York, London, Berlin, Austin, and Bangalore.

How early can I find out about a startup's hiring plans? ▼

Our data shows that engineering hiring bursts on GitHub precede public job postings by an average of 2–3 weeks. This gives recruiters a time advantage to identify and reach out to candidates before competing firms begin sourcing.
