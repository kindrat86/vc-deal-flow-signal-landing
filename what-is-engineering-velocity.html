What Is Engineering Velocity?

GitDealFlow · VC Deal Flow Signal · Q3 2026

Quick Answer

Engineering velocity is a metric that measures how quickly and effectively a startup's engineering team ships code. It is typically quantified through commit frequency (commits per week), contributor growth rate, and repository expansion over a rolling 14-day window. GitDealFlow tracks these signals across 324+ venture-backed startups to surface accelerating teams before they raise their next round.

Detailed Explanation

Engineering velocity goes beyond simple "lines of code" metrics. GitDealFlow measures three core dimensions: (1) Commit velocity — the raw number of commits pushed to default branches over a trailing 14-day period, which reveals how actively a team is developing; (2) Contributor growth — the rate at which new developers are committing code, indicating team expansion and organizational investment; and (3) Repository expansion — the creation of new repositories or significant architectural changes that suggest platform buildout rather than incremental maintenance. Each startup in the VC Deal Flow Signal dataset receives a velocity score normalized against its sector peers. A startup in the 90th percentile of commit velocity is classified as "accelerating" — historically a leading indicator that a fundraise is 3–6 weeks away. Velocity data is refreshed weekly from public GitHub activity across 15 sectors including healthcare, enterprise SaaS, data infrastructure, and web3.

Why It Matters for VCs and Angel Investors

VCs and angel investors use engineering velocity to identify startups that are building faster than their peers — a leading indicator of product-market fit and future fundraising success. A team that ships code at 3x the sector average is often 6–8 weeks ahead of the market in recognizing their trajectory.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is engineering velocity calculated?

Engineering velocity is calculated from three GitHub-derived metrics over a 14-day rolling window: commit frequency, contributor count growth, and new repository creation. These are combined into a normalized velocity score per startup.

Why does engineering velocity matter for VCs?

Engineering velocity is a leading indicator of startup momentum. Teams that accelerate their code output 3–6 weeks before a fundraise show characteristic patterns that GitDealFlow detects — giving investors a data edge over traditional sourcing channels.

What is a good engineering velocity score?

A good velocity score varies by sector. In enterprise SaaS, the average is 12–18 commits per 14 days; in web3, 8–14; in healthcare, 8–15. Top-quartile startups typically show 3x the sector average plus positive contributor growth.

How often is velocity data updated?

Velocity data is refreshed weekly from public GitHub APIs. Each startup's commit activity, contributor changes, and repository updates are recalculated every 7 days.

Yes. Use the GitDealFlow Velocity Checker tool at /check-velocity to look up any GitHub organization's commit cadence and acceleration signals in real-time.
