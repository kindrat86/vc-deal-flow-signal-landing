Scout Score

Scout Score
A 0-100 GitHub taste metric that measures how closely a GitHub user's starring history correlates with ~75 validated unicorn startups. Higher scores indicate better deal-sourcing taste — the ability to spot breakout companies before they raise large rounds. Computed via the Scout Receipts tool on signals.gitdealflow.com.
↗ Compute your Scout Score at gitdealflow.com/scout

Commit Velocity

Commit Velocity
The rate of Git commits pushed to a startup's GitHub organization repositories in a trailing 14-day rolling window. Higher commit velocity indicates increasing engineering output. This is the foundational metric in the GitDealFlow signal methodology and a primary input to the Velocity Score.
↗ Learn more at gitdealflow.com/engineering-velocity-guide · View live data

Deal Flow

Deal Flow
The pipeline of startup investment opportunities that venture capitalists and angel investors evaluate over a given period. GitDealFlow enhances traditional deal flow by surfacing engineering-accelerating startups 21-47 days before fundraise announcements, giving investors a proprietary sourcing advantage.
↗ Explore at gitdealflow.com · See active signals

Engineering Velocity

Engineering Velocity
A composite measure of a startup's team output rate, combining commit velocity, contributor growth, and repository expansion into a single momentum signal. Used by GitDealFlow to rank and compare startup engineering acceleration across sectors and funding stages.
↗ Read the guide · Browse rankings

Signal Type

Signal Type
One of four classifications of engineering acceleration patterns detected in the VC Deal Flow Signal dataset: Deploy Frequency Spike, Engineering Hiring Burst, Infrastructure Buildout, or Framework Migration. Each signal type indicates a different form of startup acceleration and carries distinct implications for investors.
↗ Compare signal types · View active signals

Deploy Frequency Spike

Deploy Frequency Spike
A Signal Type indicating a sudden increase in deployment or release activity within a startup's GitHub organization. Often precedes product launches, major feature rollouts, or platform changes. Detected algorithmically by comparing current deploy cadence against the startup's historical baseline.
↗ Signal Type comparison · Live dashboard

Engineering Hiring Burst

Engineering Hiring Burst
A Signal Type detected when a startup rapidly adds multiple new committers or opens several engineering positions simultaneously. This signal often precedes a period of increased engineering velocity, as new team members ramp up their contributions.
↗ Signal Type details · See hiring signals

Infrastructure Buildout

Infrastructure Buildout
A Signal Type indicating rapid expansion of a startup's codebase through new repository creation and infrastructure-related commits. Suggests the startup is building new products, services, or scaling its technical infrastructure in preparation for growth.
↗ Signal Type comparison · Monitor buildouts

Framework Migration

Framework Migration
A Signal Type detected when a startup shows patterns consistent with technology stack changes — moving between frameworks, cloud providers, or software architectures. Framework migrations can indicate technical debt cleanup, platform modernization, or preparation for scale.
↗ Signal Type details · View migrations

Contributor Growth

Contributor Growth
The rate at which unique committers join a startup's GitHub organization over a defined period. Positive contributor growth signals team expansion and increasing engineering investment. GitDealFlow tracks contributor growth across 4,200+ startup orgs to identify hiring trends.
↗ Browse contributor data

Breakout Signal

Breakout Signal
The strongest form of engineering acceleration detected by GitDealFlow. A breakout signal indicates a startup whose engineering activity crosses all empirical thresholds across multiple signal types simultaneously. These startups typically show the strongest correlation with imminent fundraise announcements.
↗ See breakout signals live

Seed Stage

Seed Stage
The early startup funding phase where companies have demonstrated product-market fit and are scaling their engineering team. Seed-stage startups often show contributor growth and initial velocity signals. GitDealFlow tracks seed-stage startups to identify those accelerating toward Series A.
↗ Explore startup stages · View seed signals

Series A/B

Series A / Series B
Growth-stage startup funding rounds where companies with proven traction raise significant capital from institutional investors. Series A/B startups in the GitDealFlow dataset typically display strong engineering velocity, multiple active signal types, and sustained contributor growth.
↗ View stages · Series A/B signals

Growth Stage

Growth Stage
Late-stage startup funding phase (Series C and beyond) characterized by rapid scaling, substantial engineering teams (often 50+ contributors), and consistent velocity across multiple GitHub organizations. Growth-stage startups in the dataset show the highest absolute commit volumes.
↗ Explore stages · Growth-stage data

GitHub Velocity

GitHub Velocity
A commit rate metric measuring the raw number of GitHub commits pushed by a startup's organization in a defined time window. GitHub Velocity is the foundational input for the Engineering Velocity Index and feeds into the composite Velocity Score computation.

Pre-seed

Pre-seed
The earliest startup funding phase, often occurring before formal incorporation or product launch. Pre-seed startups on GitHub may show initial commit activity, early repository creation, and founding-team contributions. GitDealFlow's pre-seed signals help investors identify promising teams at the very beginning of their journey.

Engineering Momentum

Engineering Momentum
A sustained trend in engineering velocity over multiple 14-day observation windows. Unlike a temporary spike, momentum indicates consistent investment in product development and team growth. GitDealFlow tracks momentum trends to differentiate between one-time bursts and genuine acceleration.

SSRN 6606558

SSRN 6606558 — VC Deal Flow Signal
The canonical research paper that validates the VC Deal Flow Signal methodology. Published on the Social Science Research Network (SSRN), the paper demonstrates that engineering velocity spikes on GitHub precede fundraise announcements by a median of 31 days, validated against 219 documented fundraise events. The preprint is available at ssrn.com/abstract=6606558.

Open Source Intelligence (OSINT) in VC
The practice of using publicly available data sources for deal sourcing and startup evaluation in venture capital. GitDealFlow's engineering velocity dataset is a leading example of OSINT in VC — extracting investment signals from public GitHub activity that anyone can access and verify.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are these startup and VC terms defined?

Each term in this glossary is defined based on its usage in the GitDealFlow VC Deal Flow Signal dataset and methodology. The core research is published as SSRN preprint 6606558, which validates the relationship between engineering velocity on GitHub and subsequent fundraise announcements. Terms like Scout Score, Velocity Score, and Signal Types are operationalized in the live dataset at signals.gitdealflow.com.

Who maintains this glossary?

This glossary is maintained by The Data Nerd, the pseudonymous author and operator of GitDealFlow and the VC Deal Flow Signal research project. The maintainer publishes weekly signal digests, methodology updates, and sector analysis. Contact signals@gitdealflow.com for corrections or suggestions.

How often is the glossary updated?

The glossary is reviewed and updated quarterly to reflect new signal types, methodology refinements, and emerging startup ecosystem terms. The underlying signal data at signals.gitdealflow.com is updated weekly every Sunday. The research methodology is versioned through SSRN preprint 6606558 and Zenodo archive DOI updates.
