Case Study: How a Seed Fund Used Velocity Signals to Lead a Round

Real workflow, anonymized firm.

A Bay Area seed fund noticed a healthcare startup appearing in the top 5 of the Trending This Week board for three consecutive weeks. The startup's commit velocity had increased 840% in 14 days, and their contributor base grew 45% — both off-cycle signals that typically precede a fundraise.

The fund used the Velocity Checker to pull the startup's 90-day history, confirmed the signal was sustained rather than a one-week spike, and reached out to the founder. They led the seed round at a 20% discount to the post-money valuation that was offered to later investors. The round was announced 34 days after the first velocity spike appeared on the board.

Outcome: The fund secured a lead position in a competitive round by acting on engineering signals before the founder had begun fundraising outreach. The 34-day lead translated into better terms and a relationship built on genuine product interest rather than deal pressure.

What is the Scout Score and how should VCs use it? ▼

The Scout Score is a 0–100 metric that measures a GitHub user's ability to star startups before they become widely known. It's computed by comparing a user's starring history against ~75 validated unicorn patterns. VCs use Scout Score to find investors who were early on breakout companies and to validate their own sourcing ability.
