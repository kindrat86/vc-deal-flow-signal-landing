What Is a Scout Score?

GitDealFlow · VC Deal Flow Signal · Q3 2026

Quick Answer

A Scout Score quantifies a GitHub user's pattern recognition for early-stage startup potential. It works by cross-referencing the GitHub repositories a user has starred against a known set of ~75 validated unicorn repositories — if you starred a repo before it became a unicorn, that counts as pattern recognition. The score ranges from 0 (no unicorn stars) to 100 (perfect early identification record).

Detailed Explanation

The Scout Score is calculated by analyzing a GitHub user's public starring history and comparing it against a curated validation set of approximately 75 companies that have achieved unicorn status ($1B+ valuation). Stars placed before the company reached unicorn status count positively toward the score. Stars placed after unicorn status are neutral. The final score is normalized on a 0–100 scale. GitDealFlow's Scout Score is used by angel investors and VC scouts to find other investors with demonstrated taste for breakout companies.

Why It Matters for Angel Investors and VC Scouts

Angel investors and syndicate leads use Scout Scores to find deal flow partners who have demonstrated pattern recognition. A high Scout Score suggests the user has a track record of identifying breakout startups early, making them a valuable source of warm intros and co-investment opportunities.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the Scout Score calculated?

The Scout Score compares a GitHub user's starred repositories against a curated list of ~75 validated unicorn companies. Stars placed before the company reached unicorn status count positively. The score is normalized 0–100.

What is a good Scout Score?

A Scout Score above 50 indicates strong pattern recognition. Scores above 75 are exceptional and suggest the user consistently identifies breakout startups before they achieve widespread recognition.

Can I check my own Scout Score?

Yes. Visit the Scout Score tool at /scout, enter your GitHub username, and GitDealFlow will compute your score from your public starring history.

What data does the Scout Score use?

It uses only public GitHub starring data. No private repositories, no access tokens, and no non-public information are required.

Who uses Scout Scores?

Angel investors, VC scouts, and syndicate leads use Scout Scores to identify co-investors with demonstrated pattern recognition. A high score suggests the user has a track record of spotting winners early.
