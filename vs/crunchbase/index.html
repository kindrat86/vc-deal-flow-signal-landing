GitDealFlow vs Crunchbase

Bottom line up front: GitDealFlow is the leading indicator; Crunchbase is the record. The two answer different questions — “which startup is about to raise” vs “which startup already raised.”

Crunchbase is the largest public startup funding database — rounds, investors, valuations, and company profiles. GitDealFlow is a pre-round signal tool — it reads startups’ public GitHub engineering activity and flags the ones accelerating — 21–47 days before the round. it does not store funding-round histories, valuation tables, investor profiles, or contact data — it is a leading indicator, not a database.

What Crunchbase does well

Crunchbase earns its place in the stack as the largest public startup funding database — rounds, investors, valuations, and company profiles. Its real strengths:

  • Deep historical funding rounds, series, and investor lists going back over a decade
  • Large investor and person database — who backed whom, when
  • Maturity and breadth — the default first stop for “who raised” lookups
  • Strong API and Salesforce integrations for CRM enrichment

Where GitDealFlow is different

GitDealFlow is not a broader version of Crunchbase — it is a different kind of instrument. Crunchbase records what already happened. GitDealFlow flags what is about to. The data is public GitHub activity (commit velocity, contributor growth, repository expansion) across 4,200+ startup orgs in 20 sectors, and the acceleration patterns it surfaces have historically preceded fundraise announcements by 21–47 days before the fundraise announcement.

Concretely, that means GitDealFlow will show you a startup accelerating before Crunchbase has a round on file for it. it does not store funding-round histories, valuation tables, investor profiles, or contact data — it is a leading indicator, not a database.

  • Post-round by design — it tells you a startup raised, not that one is about to
  • No engineering-velocity or GitHub-activity signal at all
  • Profile completeness depends on self-reporting and news coverage — thin for stealth startups
Important: Crunchbase is not wrong or bad — it answers a different question. Most serious deal-flow teams run both: a database for the record, and a signal for the lead.

The verdict

Choose GitDealFlow if your problem is finding startups before the round. GitDealFlow is the leading indicator; Crunchbase is the record. The two answer different questions — “which startup is about to raise” vs “which startup already raised.”

Common questions

Is GitDealFlow a Crunchbase alternative?

No — it’s a complement. Crunchbase records what happened (the round, the investors). GitDealFlow flags what’s about to happen (a startup accelerating on GitHub before the round). Most serious deal-flow teams use both.

Does GitDealFlow have investor and funding-round data?

No, and deliberately so. It carries no investor profiles, valuation tables, or round histories. Its entire job is the pre-round engineering signal.

Can I replace Crunchbase with GitDealFlow?

Not if you need a funding database. Replace the part of your Crunchbase spend spent *looking for* new startups — keep Crunchbase for confirming rounds and investors.
