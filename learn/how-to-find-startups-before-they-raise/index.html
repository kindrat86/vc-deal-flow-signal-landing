How to find startups before they raise

The investors who win are the ones who see startups in week two, not week ten. Here's a concrete playbook for finding startups before the round is announced — using engineering signals, hiring intent, and network, in that order.

The step-by-step playbook

  1. Track engineering acceleration on GitHub — Start with a quantitative leading indicator. GitDealFlow reads commit velocity, contributor growth, and repo expansion across 4,200+ startup orgs and sends you five accelerating startups every Sunday — 21–47 days before the round. This is the cheapest, earliest signal available.
  2. Layer hiring intent on top — Watch for spikes in specialized job postings (senior ML engineers, infra engineers, etc.). A startup hiring aggressively is preparing to build — often before a raise. Tools: LinkedIn jobs, the startup's own careers page.
  3. Read the technical press and community — Hacker News 'Show HN', specialized subreddits (r/machinelearning, r/devops), and niche newsletters surface technical breakthroughs before mainstream press. These correlate with acceleration.
  4. Map the network — Who's the founder? Who are the early investors? Which accelerator (if any)? Founder track record and early backer quality are powerful but require being in or near the network.
  5. Confirm with post-round data — Once a startup is on your radar, use Crunchbase or PitchBook to check round history, investors, and valuations. This confirms — it doesn't discover.
  6. Build a repeatable weekly cadence — Deal flow is a habit, not a sprint. Most serious investors run a fixed weekly cadence: read the GitDealFlow digest Monday, filter for fit Tuesday–Thursday, take meetings Friday.

Common questions

Can you really predict fundraises?

Not with certainty — but engineering acceleration is a strong leading indicator. GitDealFlow's signal has historically preceded rounds by 21–47 days, validated against 219 documented fundraises. It's a probability boost, not a crystal ball.

Do I need to be technical to find startups early?

No. Tools like GitDealFlow do the engineering analysis for you — each startup comes with a plain-English note on why it's accelerating. You read the signal, you don't read the code.

Is GitHub the only early signal?

No, but it's the most scalable and quantitative. Hiring intent, founder network, and technical press also work. The advantage of GitHub is that it's public, continuous, and measurable.
