Why Velocity Data Wins

Engineering velocity is a leading indicator of startup momentum. When a startup's commit velocity spikes, contributor count grows, or infrastructure buildout accelerates, something is changing inside that company — new product push, scaling team, or pre-fundraise preparation. GitDealFlow captures these signals 3–6 weeks before they appear in traditional databases.

Traditional platforms like AngelList provide valuable historical data — funding rounds, valuations, team size — but they cannot tell you what's changing right now. That's the velocity gap GitDealFlow fills.

When to Use GitDealFlow

  • You want to discover startups accelerating before their next round
  • You need weekly-updated engineering signals, not quarterly data
  • You're an angel investor, scout, or small fund with a limited budget
  • You want a free public API to integrate velocity data into your own tools

When to Use AngelList

  • You need company background, valuation history, or investor lists
  • You're conducting deep due diligence on a specific startup
  • You want to track an entire asset class (PE, VC, M&A) in one platform
  • Your firm already has a institutional data infrastructure budget

Frequently Asked Questions

No. GitDealFlow carries no valuations, deal terms, or fund performance data. It is purely an engineering-acceleration signal based on public GitHub activity.

Do I need both GitDealFlow and AngelList?

Many power users combine them. Use AngelList for company background and historical data; use GitDealFlow for the forward-looking engineering velocity signal.
