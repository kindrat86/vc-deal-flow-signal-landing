For micro VCs

Micro VC funds ($10–50M) compete on early access and thesis fit. GitDealFlow’s pre-round engineering signal gives analysts a systematic discovery layer without institutional pricing.

Why micro VCs use GitDealFlow

Micro VCs (funds under $50M) face the same deal-flow problem as larger funds but without the infrastructure budget. PitchBook is $20k+/year/seat; Crunchbase Pro starts at $29–$55/month. GitDealFlow’s free Signal Digest (five accelerating startups every Sunday) plus the €49/month Dashboard gives a micro VC a systematic pre-round signal for less than the cost of one coffee per week.

The micro VC stack

Most micro VCs run: GitDealFlow (free, pre-round discovery) + Crunchbase Pro ($29–$55/month, post-round confirmation) + a lightweight CRM (Affinity, Notion, or Airtable). Total stack: under $100/month per analyst.

The Dashboard tier adds ranked filters by sector, stage, and geography — enough for a small fund to run a disciplined pipeline without enterprise overhead.

Common questions

Is GitDealFlow enough for a micro VC on its own?

For pre-round discovery, yes. You’ll still want Crunchbase or PitchBook for post-round confirmation and investor mapping. The free tier + a cheap database covers most micro VC needs.

How does this compare to Tracxn?

Tracxn starts at ~$5k–$8k/year — often more than micro VCs want to spend at entry. GitDealFlow’s free tier + $0–197/month dashboard is the lower-cost signal layer. See /cost-of/tracxn-pricing for the full comparison.

Can micro VCs use the API?

Yes — the Insider Circle tier (€197/month) includes API access for integration into internal tools like Airtable and Notion.
